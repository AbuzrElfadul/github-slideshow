# Format Analysis — "ALL OF PHYSICS explained in 14 Minutes" (Wacky Science)

**Reference video:** https://youtu.be/ZAqIoDhornk
**Channel:** Wacky Science — https://www.youtube.com/@wacky.science
**Sibling videos in the same series:** COMPUTER SCIENCE in 17 Minutes, BIOLOGY in 17 Minutes,
GENERAL CHEMISTRY in 19 Minutes.

> Sourcing note: YouTube is blocked from this environment, so this analysis is built from the
> video/channel metadata retrievable via search plus the series' observable format conventions —
> not from a frame-by-frame watch or a transcript. The structural read below is a reconstruction
> of the format, and the scripts in this folder are original writing in that format.

---

## 1. The channel in one sentence

A young creator compresses an entire academic discipline — the kind that normally takes a
multi-year degree — into a single sub-20-minute video, narrated fast, animated cheaply, and
joked through constantly.

## 2. Why the format works

The format solves four hard problems at once:

**Demand is pre-validated.** Physics, chemistry, biology, computer science, and math are
*compulsory subjects*. Millions of people are actively required to learn them and are actively
failing to. You are not manufacturing interest — you are intercepting existing, permanent,
high-volume search intent. These videos don't decay; they accumulate.

**The outline problem is already solved.** A curriculum is a pedagogical ordering refined over
centuries: each concept is arranged to need only the concepts before it. You do not have to
invent a structure. You inherit one that is known to work, and your only job is compression.

**The hook is a ratio, not a story.** Most content has to earn attention with narrative. This
format earns it arithmetically: *four years : fourteen minutes*. The viewer can evaluate the
offer instantly, and the offer is absurd enough to be interesting and plausible enough to be
credible.

**"ALL" is a retention device disguised as a title word.** A completionist promise opens a loop
that only closes at the end of the runtime. Bailing at 40% means knowingly leaving with 60% of
physics missing. Compare "5 Physics Concepts Explained" — nothing is lost by leaving early.

## 3. Title engineering

    [ENTIRE FIELD IN CAPS] explained in [ODD NUMBER] Minutes

Three deliberate choices:

- **All-caps subject** — signals the *scale* of the promise, which is the product.
- **An odd, unrounded number** — 14, 17, 19. A rounded number ("in 15 minutes") reads as a
  marketing estimate. An odd one reads as a measurement: *this is how long it actually took*.
  It buys credibility for free.
- **"explained", not "learn" or "guide"** — matches how people actually type the query.

Thumbnail carries the same load: the big number, a handful of instantly-legible field icons
(atom, DNA helix, E=mc²), high contrast, minimal text.

## 4. Hook architecture — three layers

**Layer 1 — Title + thumbnail.** This is 90% of the hook and it does its work before the video
plays. Governing rule: *the promise must be slightly unbelievable but never a lie.* "ALL OF
PHYSICS in 14 minutes" is at the edge. "ALL OF PHYSICS in 4 minutes" crosses it, and the
audience punishes the miss in the comments.

**Layer 2 — The first 10 seconds.** No branding, no "hey guys, welcome back", no channel intro.
Concept one begins almost immediately. Three cold-open patterns that fit the format:

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Straight in | Skip the frame entirely, start teaching | "Everything falls. Newton worked out why." |
| Stakes frame | Name the absurd scope, then accept it | "Physics is the study of literally everything. So — all of it. Now." |
| The contract | State exactly what the viewer will own by the end | "In fourteen minutes you'll know more physics than you did after two years of school." |

**Layer 3 — Micro-hooks every 60–90 seconds.** This is the actual retention engine, and it is
the part most imitators miss. **Sections do not end on a conclusion. They end on the hook for
the next section.** The joins are load-bearing:

- "But there's a problem with that."
- "This works perfectly — until you go small enough."
- "Which is where it stops being reasonable."
- "Everyone assumed that was a pointless game. Then Einstein used it."

Write these *before* you write the section bodies. If two adjacent sections can be swapped
without breaking the joins, the ladder isn't built properly.

## 5. The scenario template — "the ladder"

    0:00–0:10   COLD OPEN — state the impossible promise
    0:10–1:00   RUNG 1 — the single most intuitive idea in the field. Zero prerequisites.
    ...         RUNGS 2–10 — 60–90s each. Each needs only what came before.
    last 1:30   THE PAYOFF RUNG — the field's biggest open mystery or its strangest result
    last 0:30   RECAP LIST + hook into the next video

Ordering law: **intuitive → counterintuitive → mind-blowing.** Never end on housekeeping. Save
the single most unsettling true fact in the discipline for the final rung — that is what gets
the video shared.

The reference video's ladder follows the classical physics curriculum almost exactly: gravity →
mass vs. weight → kinetic & potential energy → conservation → thermodynamics & entropy →
current, voltage, resistance → magnetism → Maxwell → modern physics. That is the degree
sequence. That is the point.

## 6. Voice rules

- **Second person, present tense.** "You've never seen the mass of the Earth. We know it anyway."
- **Concrete before abstract, always.** Pebbles in a bag before "bijection". A machine with a
  hopper before "function".
- **Jokes are interjections, never detours.** One clause, then straight back to the physics. A
  joke that costs five seconds of teaching is too expensive at this density.
- **Admit the weirdness.** "Feels wrong. It isn't." Acknowledging that a result is
  counterintuitive earns more trust than smoothing it over.
- **Never say "as we'll see later."** Forward references leak tension. Each rung must feel
  complete on its own.
- **Pace target:** ~150–165 wpm. A 15-minute script is roughly 2,300–2,500 words.

## 7. Where the format is weakest — and how to beat it

The failure mode of this genre is **listing rather than laddering**: ten disconnected facts
narrated quickly. It gets clicks and loses retention around minute four, because once the viewer
notices the sections are interchangeable, the completionist loop breaks.

The defence is the join. Every section must *create the need* for the next one. In the MATH
script in this folder, that chain is: counting breaks → so we invent numbers → numbers we can't
see → so we reason about relationships → relationships need proof → proof needs change →
change needs limits → and so on. The viewer is never choosing to continue; they're following a
consequence.
