You are being rebuilt right now. Millions of cells die every minute, and an unpaid repair crew replaces each one.
That crew started before you were born and has not stopped since. So why does your body eventually lose this fight?
Every answer begins with copying. A cell divides, duplicates its instructions, and passes a complete set to both new halves.
Each duplication costs something. Protective caps called telomeres sit at the ends of your chromosomes, and they shrink a little every time.
Wear them down far enough and the cell simply refuses to divide again. Biologists call that hard ceiling the Hayflick limit.
Meanwhile radiation and your own metabolism snap DNA constantly. Enzymes stitch the breaks shut, quickly and accurately, but never quite perfectly.
Badly damaged cells face a choice. Die cleanly, or shut down and refuse to leave. Biology calls those stubborn ones senescent.
They are alive, useless, and worse than idle. Senescent cells leak inflammatory chemicals that break down the healthy tissue around them.
Scientists named this inflammaging. Your mitochondria fade at the same time, so less energy reaches a repair system already falling behind.
So why did evolution never fix this? Because it did not design you to last. It shaped you to reproduce, then stopped caring.
Alter the pressure and lifespans shift. Naked mole rats reach thirty years underground, and one jellyfish reverts to its juvenile form entirely.
Which leaves the real headline. Ageing is not a law of physics. It is an engineering problem nobody was ever paid to solve.
